How is cryptocurrency taxed?

Most tax authorities treat cryptocurrency as property rather than currency. That means two main types of tax can apply to your crypto activity:

  • Capital gains tax — charged when you dispose of crypto by selling it, swapping one coin for another, or spending it. Your gain is the difference between what you received and your original cost basis.
  • Income tax — charged when you receive crypto as earnings, for example from staking rewards, mining, interest, airdrops, or being paid in crypto. This is usually taxed at your ordinary income rate.

Simply buying and holding crypto is generally not taxable. Tax is triggered when you dispose of it or earn it.

Short-term vs long-term gains

In the United States, crypto held for one year or less is taxed at higher short-term rates equal to your ordinary income bracket, while crypto held longer than a year qualifies for lower long-term capital gains rates. In Canada, only 50% of a capital gain is taxable, with no distinction based on holding period.

Cost basis methods: FIFO, LIFO and HIFO

When you sell only part of your holdings, the cost basis method determines which units you are deemed to have sold:

  • FIFO (First In, First Out) — the oldest coins are sold first. This is the most common and widely accepted method.
  • LIFO (Last In, First Out) — the most recently bought coins are sold first.
  • HIFO (Highest In, First Out) — the most expensive coins are sold first, which can reduce your taxable gain.

How to reduce your crypto tax legally

  • Hold assets longer than a year (US) to qualify for lower long-term rates.
  • Use tax-loss harvesting to offset gains with realised losses.
  • Keep detailed records of every transaction, including fees.
  • Consider tax-advantaged accounts where crypto exposure is permitted.

Frequently asked questions

How is cryptocurrency taxed?

In most countries, including the US and Canada, selling, swapping or spending crypto is a taxable event subject to capital gains tax. Crypto earned from staking, mining, interest or as payment is usually taxed as income at your ordinary rate.

Do I have to pay tax if I only hold crypto?

No. Simply buying and holding cryptocurrency is generally not a taxable event. Tax is typically triggered when you dispose of crypto by selling, trading, or spending it, or when you receive crypto as income.
