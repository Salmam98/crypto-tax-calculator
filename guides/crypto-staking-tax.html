How Is Crypto Staking Taxed? (2026 Guide)

Staking is one of the most common ways to earn passive income in crypto — but those rewards usually come with a tax bill. This guide explains, in plain English, how staking rewards are typically taxed and what records you should keep.

Staking rewards are usually taxed twice

For most investors, staking creates two separate taxable moments:

  1. When you receive the reward (income tax). In many countries, including the US and UK, staking rewards are treated as ordinary income at their fair market value on the day you gain control of them.
  2. When you later sell or swap the reward (capital gains tax). If the value has changed since you received it, you may have a capital gain or loss based on the difference between the sale price and the value you already reported as income.

How to value a staking reward

Record the fair market value (in your local currency) at the moment you can access or transfer the reward. That value becomes both your income figure and the cost basis for any future sale. Keeping a dated log of each reward makes filing far easier.

Country snapshots

United States (IRS)

Staking rewards are generally taxable as income when you gain dominion and control over them, then subject to capital gains tax when sold.

United Kingdom (HMRC)

Staking rewards are typically treated as miscellaneous income, with capital gains tax applying on later disposal.

Canada (CRA) & Australia (ATO)

Treatment can depend on whether your activity is a hobby or a business; rewards are commonly included in income with capital gains rules on disposal.

Record-keeping checklist

  • Date and time each reward was received
  • Amount of crypto received
  • Fair market value in your local currency at receipt
  • Sale date and proceeds when you dispose of it
