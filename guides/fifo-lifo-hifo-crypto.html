FIFO vs LIFO vs HIFO for Crypto Taxes

When you sell only part of your crypto, you need a rule to decide which units you sold. That rule is your cost basis method, and it directly affects how big your taxable gain is. Here are the three most common methods.

The three methods

  • FIFO (First In, First Out): the oldest coins you bought are treated as sold first. This is the default and most widely accepted method.
  • LIFO (Last In, First Out): the most recently purchased coins are treated as sold first.
  • HIFO (Highest In, First Out): the most expensive coins are treated as sold first, which often produces the smallest taxable gain.

A worked example

Imagine you bought 1 BTC at $20,000, then another 1 BTC at $40,000. Later you sell 1 BTC for $50,000.

  • FIFO: cost basis = $20,000 → taxable gain = $30,000
  • LIFO: cost basis = $40,000 → taxable gain = $10,000
  • HIFO: cost basis = $40,000 (the highest) → taxable gain = $10,000

As you can see, the method can dramatically change the gain you report in a single year.

Which method can you use?

The methods you are allowed to use depend on your country. Some jurisdictions require FIFO, while others permit specific identification (which enables HIFO) as long as you keep detailed records proving exactly which units were sold. Always confirm the rules for your country and keep thorough transaction records.

Key takeaways

  • Your cost basis method changes your taxable gain, not the total tax over the life of the asset.
  • HIFO often minimises gains in the current year, deferring tax.
  • Detailed records are essential to support whichever method you use.
